## How I read the prompt
A calm, methodical inspection rather than a showcase: a steady pace (≤ 0.4 m/s), slightly raised camera positions with a gentle downward tilt (up to 22°) so that surfaces are visible, and long holds on the food- and drink-handling areas. The route goes from a dining-room overview to close views of table tops and booths. It then stops at the service alcove at the kitchen door, where the tiled kitchen floor, white walls and storage shelving are visible. Finally it flies along the bar at 1.9 m, looking down on the prep area: ice wells, taps, stainless counters and the back-bar. It ends holding on that prep area.

**Important limitation:** the commercial kitchen itself is **not reachable**. Its doorways allow at most 0.21–0.25 m clearance (5 cm-resolution widest-path search), below the required 0.3 m. The kitchen is therefore only glimpsed through the service-alcove doorway. An inspector would want to see the kitchen, and this video cannot show it without breaking the clearance rule.

## Compared with the baseline
The baseline does enter the kitchen, but only by violating the constraints. It reaches 1.0 m/s, rolls up to 22° and pitches up to 51°, and its yaw whips at up to 473°/s. It comes closer than 0.3 m in 41% of frames (min 0.02 m). The near-field critic flags 39% of its frames as mostly blocked by geometry within 0.8 m. Ours is fully within the limits, steady, and readable, but it shows less of the back of house.

## Remaining flaws
- No kitchen interior (see above). The restrooms are not reachable either.
- The bar flight is high (1.9 m), because that is where the clearance is. It looks down onto the bar rather than across it.

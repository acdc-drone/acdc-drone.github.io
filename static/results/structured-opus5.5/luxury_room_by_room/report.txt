## How each stage went

**Stage 1 — semantic shot planning and seed retrieval.**
- Environment description: `stage1/luxury/environment_description.txt` (I wrote it from the 64 time-ordered frames that `OpenAIToolkit._video_frames` samples, viewed as 4 contact sheets; `stage1/luxury/env_frames/`). Shared by both runs of the scene (same video, same prompt).
- Waypoint descriptions: `runs/luxury_room_by_room/stage1/waypoints.json` — 14 descriptions written to `Utils.WAYPOINT_SCHEMA` (9-grid placement, ‘A beautifully taken picture.’ prefix).
- Retrieval: SigLIP2 text embeddings (`stage1/text_embeddings.npy`) vs the precomputed RADIO/SigLIP2 frame embeddings of the bag, frame stride 5, top-8 per description, de-duplicated → candidates exported from the MCAP with poses by the bundle's `export_selected_frames_from_mcap` (`stage1/retrieval.json`, `stage1/selected_frames/`).
- Select & sort: I viewed all exported candidates (`stage1/selection_sheets/`) and answered in the `select_frames` JSON schema (`stage1/selection_response.json`); 14 shots kept, none omitted. Seeds = recorded position + yaw of each selected frame; recorded pitch/roll discarded (`stage1/seeds.json`).

**Stage 2 — visual preference refinement.**
- 1400 pairwise comparisons in total (B = 100 per shot, M = 64 candidates, ρ_trans = 1 m, ρ_rot = 30°, GP kernel l_rot = 15°, l_trans = 0.5 m, σ_f = 1, jitter 1e-6, Laplace/Bradley–Terry posterior and dueling Thompson sampling from `pose_main.py`).
- Challenger accepted 304 times, ties ({3}, no pair added — code behaviour) 1 times. Mean displacement from seed 0.87 m (max 1.88 m); mean |yaw change| 14.9°.
- Every comparison is recorded: the exact sheet image I judged (`sheets/luxury/round_*.jpg`), the answers I gave (`answers/luxury/rNNN.json`), and per shot `runs/luxury_room_by_room/stage2/wp_XX/state.json` (all poses, pairs, Thompson-sampling info, displayed left/right order, decision, incumbent after each step). Refined poses: `stage2/all_best_results.json` (degrees).

**Stage 3 — physical trajectory generation** (bundle `run_paper_scenes.py --resume --skip-video --poses-json ... --pose-angle-unit degrees`, i.e. the unchanged A* → tunnel → TOGT → validation path).
- A* path 395 points; TOGT duration 151.1 s, path length 92.3 m; bundle validation passed; no re-optimisation needed.
- Camera orientation: the runner’s RotationSpline through the refined yaw-only orientations at the actual gate arrival times (for pure-yaw rotations this is a C² cubic interpolation of the unwrapped yaw, as in the paper).
- Video: `tools/render_final.py` → `scene_patch/renderer/render_patched_flythrough.py` (unchanged) with `render_settings.json`, patched mesh, same unsharp mask and libx264 CRF 18 encode as `make_video.py`.

## Deviations from ACDC’s design (and why)

- **VLM**: every VLM call (environment description, waypoint generation, frame select/sort, 100 comparisons per shot) was made by me (Claude Opus 5.5) from the same inputs, instead of `gpt-6-astra`. Images were shown to me as labelled contact sheets (64 env frames at 480×270; candidates at 480×270; each comparison pair at 400×225 per image, six pairs per sheet) rather than one API request per call at 960 px; the decision format is the code’s (1/2/3). Left/right order of incumbent vs challenger was randomised per pair and mapped back to the code’s `compare_images(best, cand)` contract.
- **Number of shots**: 14 descriptions → 14 shots, against 31 shots in the saved ACDC run. I wrote the descriptions after estimating the cost of 100 comparisons per shot, so I cannot rule out that this pushed me towards fewer shots than `gpt-6-astra` produced under the same “more waypoints is better” prompt.
- **Refinement search space follows the paper, not `pose_main.py`’s sampler**: yaw-only perturbation (Δθ ~ U[−30°, 30°]) plus translation u ~ U(S²), r ~ U[0, 1 m], height clamped to [0.3, 2.5] m (my choice; the paper gives no z-range), roll = pitch = 0. The code’s `_sample_orientation_near` rotates about a random 3-D axis (introducing roll/pitch) and `refine_poses.py` uses 32 candidates; the paper specifies yaw-only, M = 64. The GP, Laplace update, Thompson draw and the tie rule are the code’s functions (kernel evaluated vectorised from iteration 99 of cottage onward, numerically identical to 1e-16).
- **Refinement renderer**: a persistent Blender process with exactly the capture settings of `render_blender_flythrough.py` (960×540, 8 spp, 80° FOV, flat 0.05 grey world, original mesh), on GPU instead of CPU, so the mesh is loaded once instead of per image. Restaurant used the original mesh under my 1.5 GB cap; luxury ran through the shared heavy queue.
- **Text encoder**: the RADIO SigLIP2 adaptor’s text tower (`google/siglip2-so400m-patch16-naflex` `text_model`, the 1152-d space of the saved frame embeddings) loaded on its own in fp16 (streamed per tensor; only the needed token-embedding rows on GPU) because the full RADIO checkpoint does not fit the 2 GB cap. Tokenisation copied verbatim from `siglip2_adaptor.py`. The weights were deleted afterwards for the disk limit (re-fetch with `tools/fetch_siglip2_text.py`).
- **Candidates per description**: 8 (paper and the saved c8 runs) rather than the code default of 3.
- **Disk limit (FEEDBACK 03:25)**: after each scene finished, the full-resolution per-candidate refinement renders were deleted except the seed and the final incumbent; every judged pair is still kept as the sheet image I actually saw, and all poses are in `state.json`, so any render can be regenerated. Exported candidate frames were re-encoded PNG→JPEG q92.
- **Final video render** streamed in 300-frame chunks (PNG chunks deleted after encoding) instead of writing all frames first; same renderer and settings.

Notes: clearance is the distance from the trajectory centreline (sampled every 5 ms) to the surface of the nearest occupied 0.2 m voxel of the same occupancy grid the planner used. ACDC’s tunnel margins are 0.01 m and its validator only checks that the centreline is not inside an occupied voxel, so ≥ 0.3 m is not something the design aims for. TOGT treats the 1 m/s limit as a soft penalty (the bundle itself records `velocity_limit_is_soft_penalty: true`). Camera roll/pitch are those of the exported CSV quaternions; body tilt/thrust/rates are derived from the TOGT polynomial with the drolib flatness convention.

## Main flaws I saw

- The prompt names rooms the scan does not have (formal dining room, piano room, staircase, two-storey great room, walk-in closet); I mapped them to the nearest existing views (kitchen island, lounge corner, great room, a bedroom corner). The 'Walk-in closet' shot is really a guest-bedroom corner.
- Between the kitchen and the primary suite the A* route leaves the house over the terrace, so the video shows broken exterior scan geometry for several seconds; there are also dark stone-wall and veneer close-ups in transit.
